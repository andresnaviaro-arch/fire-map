# Week 3 — A: Audit

**Goal:** map how the work *actually* happens — not how the procedure manual
says it happens. The audit is the foundation for every later decision; a
redesign built on an idealized process fails on contact with reality.

**Prerequisite:** your completed workflow inventory
(`templates/00-workflow-inventory.md`). Audit the top 3–5 candidates from it in
depth; you will pick one in Week 4.

## The audit questions

Work through all fifteen for each candidate workflow:

1. What **triggers** the workflow?
2. What **outcome** is it meant to produce?
3. **Who participates?**
4. What **tasks** does each person perform?
5. What **systems** do they use?
6. What **information** enters and leaves each step?
7. Where are **decisions** made?
8. Which decisions depend on **undocumented experience**? (These are the hard part — tribal knowledge about which customers need what, which quotes need a margin check, which projects are real.)
9. What **handoffs** occur?
10. Where does work **wait**?
11. What gets **duplicated**?
12. What are the common **exceptions**?
13. What causes **errors or rework**?
14. Who **owns the final outcome**?
15. What happens if the workflow **fails**?

## Evidence to collect

Do not audit from memory alone. Gather:

- Interviews (even 20 minutes with inside sales, pricing, or a fellow AM)
- Screen recordings of yourself doing the work once, at normal speed
- Process documents (and note where reality diverges from them)
- Email examples — real threads, sanitized
- Spreadsheets actually used to run the process
- CRM records for representative opportunities
- Reports the workflow feeds
- **Three case types**: a typical case, a difficult case, a failure case
- Approximate volumes and processing times (per week/month; minutes per instance)

> Handle real customer data carefully from the start: collect it into approved
> company locations only, and redact names/pricing in anything that leaves them.
> The habits you build here become the governance story you tell in Week 12.

## Deliverables

Produce these seven artifacts (a page or a table each is enough):

1. **Current-state process map** — boxes and arrows, swimlanes per participant, from trigger to outcome, including the waits and loops that really happen.
2. **Roles and responsibilities matrix** — who does, who decides, who approves, who is informed, per step.
3. **Systems and data inventory** — every system touched; what is read/written; structured or unstructured; system of record or copy.
4. **Decision inventory** — every decision point; inputs; rules vs. judgment; who makes it; consequence of getting it wrong.
5. **Bottleneck register** — where work waits, for how long, and why.
6. **Exception catalogue** — the recurring "special cases" and how each is actually handled today.
7. **Baseline performance table** — volume, cycle time, touch time, error/rework rate, and outcome rate (e.g., quote-to-order conversion) as best you can estimate them. **This is the "before" picture every later claim of improvement will be measured against — do not skip it.**

## Quality test

Show the process map to someone who does the work. If they say "yes, and you
even caught the annoying part," the audit is done. If they say "well,
officially, yes…" — you mapped the manual, not the work. Go back.
